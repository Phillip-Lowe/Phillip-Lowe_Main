# SyStack Appointment Recovery Pilot — 15-Minute Interview Script

**Mission:** SYSTACK-NOSHOW-30D-001  
**Purpose:** Collect evidence about how appointment-based businesses manage last-minute schedule changes and recover openings.  
**Use only with GREEN or SOL authorization to contact prospects.**

---

## Opening (1–2 minutes)

“Thanks for taking the call. I’m [Name] with SyStack. This is purely research — I’m not here to sell anything. We’re talking to appointment-based businesses to understand what actually happens when someone misses an appointment, cancels late, or doesn’t respond to reminders.

I’d like to keep this to about 15 minutes. May I record this for internal note-taking? [Wait for answer.] Anything you share will be used only by our research team.

Could you start by telling me your role and how you fit into scheduling or operations at [Business Name]?”

---

## Section 1: What Happened Most Recently? (3 minutes)

1. “Think back to the most recent time an appointment did not go as planned — someone no-showed, canceled late, or never confirmed. Can you walk me through what happened?”
2. “How did you first realize there was a problem — system alert, staff noticed, customer called?”
3. “Was this a no-show, a late cancellation, or something else? Could you tell the difference at the time?”
4. “What time of day or day of week was it? Did that make it harder to react?”

**Goal:** Capture real behavior and exact customer language, not abstractions.

---

## Section 2: How Is It Handled Today? (3 minutes)

5. “After you realize an appointment is at risk, what happens next? Who does what, and in what order?”
6. “How do you currently communicate with the customer — SMS, call, email, app notification, something else?”
7. “What tools or platforms do you use for scheduling and reminders today?”
8. “Is the follow-up process the same for every appointment, or do you handle some customers differently?”

**Goal:** Document current workflow, tools, and inconsistency.

---

## Section 3: What Does It Cost? (3 minutes)

9. “About how many appointment outcomes like this do you see per week or per month?”
10. “What is the typical value of one appointment or service slot that gets lost?”
11. “When an appointment falls through, what happens to the staff time or room/equipment that was reserved?”
12. “How much staff time is spent following up, rescheduling, or managing the waitlist?”
13. “If you had to estimate, what does one missed or late-canceled appointment cost you in lost revenue, idle staff, or rebooking effort?”

**Goal:** Quantify frequency, value, manual work, and total cost.

---

## Section 4: What Has Already Been Tried? (3 minutes)

14. “What have you already tried to reduce these situations? Reminders, deposits, cancellation fees, overbooking, waitlists, calls?”
15. “What worked best? What worked least well?”
16. “Why do you think the current approach hasn’t fully solved it?”
17. “What would need to be true for you to feel confident that the problem is under control?”

**Goal:** Understand existing workarounds, partial successes, and barriers.

---

## Section 5: Decision-Making and Pilot Interest (2 minutes)

18. “If you decided to try something different, who else would need to be involved in that decision?”
19. “What evidence would you need to see before changing your current process — for example, baseline data, a controlled test, or proof that it saves time or revenue?”
20. “Would your business be willing to share baseline appointment data for a short period to measure whether a recovery system makes a difference? Or would you consider a small controlled pilot if the conditions were right?”

**Goal:** Identify the buyer, evidence standard, and potential pilot commitment.

---

## Closing (1 minute)

“That’s really helpful. I have one last question:

21. Is there anything I should have asked but didn’t?”

“Thank you for your time. If we move forward with a research pilot, may I reach back out to you? If so, what’s the best way to contact you?”

---

## Interviewer Reminders

- Avoid leading questions. Use “how,” “what,” and “walk me through.”
- Let the interviewee define the problem in their own words.
- Distinguish clearly between no-shows and late cancellations.
- Capture exact phrases the interviewee uses.
- Do not mention that SyStack reduces no-shows.
- Do not promise a free or paid pilot.
- Do not imply the business has a problem.
