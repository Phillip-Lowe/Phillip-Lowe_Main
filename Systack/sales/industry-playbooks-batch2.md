## Industry: Roofing

**Typical Company Size:** 3–25 employees; $800K–$8M annual revenue. The average roofing business employs just 2.9 people. Small companies (1–10 employees) range $500K–$2M; mid-size (11–50 employees) hit $2M–$10M.

**Common Problems:**
1. **Lead management chaos** — Storm leads flood in unpredictably; 27% average close rate industry-wide
2. **Tool silos and data fragmentation** — Average roofing company uses 5–7 different software tools (CRM, measurement, proposals, accounting, photo documentation) that don't talk to each other
3. **Manual documentation overload** — Photo-heavy workflows (before/after, insurance claims, material tracking) consume hours with no single source of truth
4. **Seasonal revenue volatility** — Storm-chasing revenue spikes then crashes; cash flow management is brutal
5. **Insurance claim coordination** — Tracking claim numbers, adjuster communications, and supplemental requests across multiple platforms

**Current Tools:**
- AccuLynx, JobNimbus, CompanyCam, Hover/EagleView (measurements), QuickBooks, Excel/spreadsheets, Google Drive/Dropbox, roofing-specific estimating tools, HOVER, RoofSnap

**Where Tools Fail:**
- CRMs don't handle storm-season lead surges or material waste tracking
- Photo tools create duplicate records with no cross-platform visibility
- Estimating tools don't integrate with accounting or CRM
- No automated follow-up for insurance claim status
- Customer portals for self-service are nonexistent or poorly connected

**Automation Opportunities:**
1. **Storm lead auto-capture and routing** — Parse hail/wind alerts, auto-enrich property data, route to right sales rep by territory
2. **Insurance claim workflow automation** — Auto-track claim numbers, trigger supplemental requests, notify customer at each status change
3. **Photo-to-CRM documentation sync** — Auto-tag CompanyCam photos to the right job record in CRM with AI classification
4. **Automated estimate follow-up sequences** — Multi-channel nurture for unsold estimates with weather-triggered re-engagement
5. **Material inventory alerts** — Track usage, auto-reorder, flag waste percentages above target

**Expected ROI:**
- Reduce admin time by 15–20 hours/week per office staff member
- Increase close rate from 27% to 35%+ through automated follow-up
- Recover $50K–$150K/year in lost leads from missed follow-ups
- Reduce photo/documentation time by 60%

**Best Offer:** SAOS **Growth Tier** ($3,500–$5,000/mo). Roofing needs multi-system integration (CRM + photo + measurement + accounting), custom workflows for storm seasons, and lead nurture automation. Starter is too thin; this requires connected systems.

**Buyer Persona:** Owner/Founder or Operations Manager at a residential roofing company doing $1M–$5M in revenue, frustrated by manual processes and ready to grow past spreadsheets.

**Decision Maker:** Owner/CEO (60%), Operations Manager (30%), Office Manager (10%)

**Discovery Questions:**
1. "How many different software tools does your team touch for a single roof job from lead to close?"
2. "What happens to a lead that comes in during storm season but doesn't close on the first visit?"
3. "Walk me through how photos from the field get into your CRM or customer file today."
4. "How do you currently track insurance claim status — manually, or does your system auto-update?"
5. "What percentage of your estimates go unsold, and do you have a structured follow-up process?"
6. "During peak season, how many leads slip through the cracks because the office is overwhelmed?"
7. "If you could automate one process in your business, what would it be?"

**Common Objections:**
1. "We already use AccuLynx/JobNimbus — why would we need SAOS?" → SAOS connects and automates *across* tools, not replaces them
2. "Our business is too seasonal — we can't afford automation year-round." → Automations scale with demand; pay for value, not headcount
3. "My guys are not tech-savvy — they won't use it." → Automation runs in the background; field staff doesn't need to change behavior
4. "We tried a CRM before and it failed." → SAOS focuses on *integration*, not rip-and-replace; iterative, not all-at-once
5. "We get leads from storm chasers — we don't need automation." → You're leaving money on the table on the leads you already paid for

## Industry: HVAC

**Typical Company Size:** 5–50 employees; $800K–$5M annual revenue. 1-truck owner/operators do $120K–$280K; 4–6 truck shops hit $800K–$1.8M; 7–15 truck companies reach $1.8M–$5M.

**Common Problems:**
1. **Missed calls = missed revenue** — 74% of calls to HVAC companies go unanswered; each missed call costs ~$1,200 in lost revenue
2. **Dispatch chaos** — Technicians spend 28% of their day driving ("windshield time"); poor routing kills margins
3. **Peak season meltdown** — Summer call volume spikes 30–40%; dispatchers enter permanent reaction mode
4. **Service agreement churn** — No automated renewal reminders or seasonal maintenance scheduling
5. **Billing bottlenecks** — Invoices bounce between billers and account managers, delaying cash flow by days or weeks

**Current Tools:**
- ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, BuildOps, FreshBooks, QuickBooks, FieldEdge, Corrigo, ServiceChannel, Verisae, Publix Portal, Slack

**Where Tools Fail:**
- Starter tools (Jobber/Housecall Pro) work for 3-tech shops but break at 10+ trucks
- Enterprise tools (ServiceTitan) cost $250–500/tech/month + $5K–$50K implementation; 6–12 month onboarding
- Customer portals for billing (Corrigo, ServiceChannel) require manual posting and monitoring
- No skill-tagged dispatch (install-certified vs service-certified vs apprentice)
- Parts-on-truck inventory isn't tied to job types in most platforms

**Automation Opportunities:**
1. **AI-powered call answering and dispatch** — 24/7 intake, smart routing by urgency/skill/location, auto-schedule
2. **Route optimization automation** — Batch jobs by neighborhood, reduce windshield time by 30%+
3. **Service agreement auto-renewal and seasonal reminders** — Prevent churn, book maintenance before peak season
4. **Portal billing automation** — Auto-create invoices from completed work orders, submit to customer portals, track rejections
5. **Emergency call triage and escalation** — After-hours emergency handling with automated technician notification and GPS dispatch

**Expected ROI:**
- Recover $120K–$250K/year in lost revenue from missed calls during peak season
- Reduce windshield time by $400K–$2.38M (depending on fleet size)
- Cut billing cycle time by 50% (faster cash flow)
- Reduce no-show rates from 12–18% to 5–7% through automated reminders

**Best Offer:** SAOS **Growth Tier** ($3,500–$5,000/mo) for 4–15 truck operations. HVAC needs multi-system dispatch coordination, portal integrations, and seasonal scaling. For owner-operators (1–3 trucks), Starter Tier ($1,500–$2,500/mo) may suffice with call routing and scheduling automation.

**Buyer Persona:** Owner/General Manager of a residential/commercial HVAC company doing $1M–$5M, drowning in dispatch and billing admin, especially during peak season.

**Decision Maker:** Owner/CEO (50%), General Manager/Operations Director (35%), Office Manager (15%)

**Discovery Questions:**
1. "What percentage of your incoming calls actually get answered and converted to booked jobs?"
2. "During your busiest week last summer, how many calls went to voicemail?"
3. "How much time do your technicians spend driving between jobs in a typical day?"
4. "Walk me through what happens when a customer calls at 8 PM with a broken AC — who handles it?"
5. "How many of your service agreements renew automatically vs. require manual outreach?"
6. "What does your billing process look like from completed job to collected payment?"
7. "If you could eliminate one administrative headache, which would disappear first?"

**Common Objections:**
1. "ServiceTitan already handles most of this." → ServiceTitan is expensive and slow to implement; SAOS layers automation *on top* of what you have
2. "We're too small to afford automation." → A 10-tech shop loses $180K+/year to missed calls and bad scheduling — automation pays for itself
3. "My dispatcher knows the routes better than any software." → Software doesn't replace judgment; it removes the manual work so dispatchers can focus on exceptions
4. "Peak season is only 3 months — why pay year-round?" → The best time to automate is *before* peak season hits, when you have time to optimize

## Industry: Plumbing

**Typical Company Size:** 3–30 employees; $700K–$4.5M annual revenue. Owner/operators at 1 truck do $110K–$250K; 4–6 truck shops hit $700K–$1.6M; 7–15 truck companies reach $1.6M–$4.5M.

**Common Problems:**
1. **Emergency dispatch overload** — Burst pipes and sewer backups don't wait for business hours; after-hours dispatch is chaotic
2. **Flat-rate pricing inconsistency** — Inconsistent quotes across technicians lead to customer complaints and lost margin
3. **Multi-property management complexity** — Commercial plumbing clients have dozens of properties; tracking history across locations is manual
4. **Callback rate eats profit** — Defective work consumes billable tech time at zero revenue
5. **Parts inventory disconnect** — Parts on the truck aren't tracked against job types; emergency runs to supply house kill billable hours

**Current Tools:**
- ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge, FreshBooks, QuickBooks, BuildOps, Zuper, OpsRev, PlumberOps (AI), dedicated plumbing CRMs

**Where Tools Fail:**
- Emergency dispatch doesn't integrate with on-call schedules or GPS location
- Flat-rate pricing books aren't connected to CRM for historical accuracy
- Multi-property commercial clients require manual cross-referencing of job history
- Parts inventory is tracked separately from job costing
- Field techs can't access complete job history or customer notes in real time

**Automation Opportunities:**
1. **Emergency call triage and auto-dispatch** — 24/7 intake, route to on-call tech by skill + proximity, auto-notify customer with ETA
2. **Flat-rate pricing automation** — Auto-generate consistent quotes from rate books, update based on material cost changes, sync to CRM
3. **Multi-property client management** — Auto-pull job history by property, flag recurring issues, schedule preventive maintenance
4. **Parts inventory and job costing sync** — Track parts usage per job, auto-reorder when stock hits threshold, calculate true job profitability
5. **Post-service follow-up and review generation** — Automated satisfaction surveys, review requests, and warranty reminders

**Expected ROI:**
- Reduce emergency response time by 40% (higher customer satisfaction, more referrals)
- Increase average ticket by 15% through consistent flat-rate pricing and upsell prompts
- Cut callback rate from industry average to top quartile through better job documentation
- Reduce parts procurement trips by 50% through predictive inventory alerts

**Best Offer:** SAOS **Growth Tier** ($3,500–$5,000/mo). Plumbing demands 24/7 availability, multi-system dispatch, and customer-facing automation. Emergency service businesses need the full integration stack.

**Buyer Persona:** Owner or General Manager of a residential/commercial plumbing company doing $1M–$4M, struggling with after-hours dispatch and inconsistent pricing.

**Decision Maker:** Owner/CEO (55%), General Manager (30%), Service Manager (15%)

**Discovery Questions:**
1. "What happens when a customer calls at 2 AM with a burst pipe — how does that call get to a technician?"
2. "How consistent are your flat-rate quotes across different technicians for the same job?"
3. "For your commercial clients with multiple properties, how do you track which location had which repair?"
4. "What's your callback rate, and how much does a callback cost you in lost billable time?"
5. "How often do your techs have to leave a job to get parts they didn't have on the truck?"
6. "How do you currently handle review generation and customer follow-up after a job is complete?"
7. "What percentage of your revenue comes from recurring maintenance agreements vs. one-time emergency calls?"

**Common Objections:**
1. "Plumbing is too hands-on for automation." → Automation handles the *scheduling, dispatch, and billing* — not the wrench work
2. "My customers call me directly, not a system." → SAOS can route calls transparently; customers never know automation is running
3. "We're a family business — tech feels impersonal." → Automation frees your family to focus on customer relationships, not paperwork
4. "Emergency calls need a human touch." → The AI triages and routes; humans handle the actual conversation and service
5. "We already have ServiceTitan." → ServiceTitan is a database, not an automation layer — SAOS makes it *do things* without manual clicks

## Industry: Dental

**Typical Company Size:** 3–15 employees per location; $700K–$1M for general practices, $1.5M–$2.5M for cosmetic/orthodontic. Solo practitioner: 3–6 staff; group practices: 10–20 staff per location.

**Common Problems:**
1. **No-shows kill revenue** — Average no-show rate 12–18%; each unfilled hygiene slot costs $150–$250; practices lose $45K–$80K/year in unfilled appointments
2. **Front desk drowning in calls** — 400–600 patient interactions/month with a 2–4 person front desk; patients wait on hold, recalls go unsent
3. **Insurance verification bottlenecks** — Rushed verification leads to claim errors, denials, and delayed payment
4. **Treatment plan follow-through** — Patients approve treatment but never schedule; no systematic follow-up
5. **Recall campaigns neglected** — Overdue patients slip through the cracks; chairs sit empty

**Current Tools:**
- Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental, Carestream Dental, tab32, Oryx Dental, DentalPrizm, Adit, Smylr, Juno (AI), LEADSORBIT, Viva AI, Patient Prism, Weave, Lighthouse 360

**Where Tools Fail:**
- Legacy PMS platforms (Dentrix, Eaglesoft) lack modern automation APIs
- Basic reminder systems are passive — patients respond but no one handles the conversation
- Insurance verification is manual, error-prone, and rushed
- Recall campaigns require manual list pulls and outreach
- Treatment plan follow-up is ad hoc, not systematic
- No 24/7 booking capability — patients call during office hours only

**Automation Opportunities:**
1. **AI-powered scheduling and cancellation backfill** — 24/7 booking via text/chat, same-day cancellation backfill with 85–92% fill rates
2. **Conversational appointment reminders and confirmations** — Two-way SMS handling reschedule requests, questions, and concerns without staff involvement
3. **Automated recall campaigns** — Monitor patient database for overdue recalls, send personalized outreach, book without staff
4. **Insurance verification automation** — Pre-verify benefits before appointments, flag coverage issues, reduce claim denials
5. **Treatment plan follow-through automation** — Systematic multi-channel follow-up for approved but unscheduled treatment plans

**Expected ROI:**
- Reduce no-shows from 12–18% to 5–7% (recover $2,800–$4,800/month in a 3-dentist practice)
- Cut administrative labor costs by 40–60%
- Increase same-day cancellation fill rate to 85–92%
- Boost recall campaign response rate by 30–50%
- Reduce insurance claim denial rate by 20–40%

**Best Offer:** SAOS **Starter Tier** ($1,500–$2,500/mo) for solo/small practices; **Growth Tier** ($3,500–$5,000/mo) for multi-location groups or DSOs. Dental is highly price-sensitive for solo practitioners but scales beautifully for groups.

**Buyer Persona:** Practice Owner/Dentist or Office Manager at a general or specialty dental practice doing $700K–$1.5M, overwhelmed by front-desk admin and no-shows.

**Decision Maker:** Practice Owner/Dentist (60%), Office Manager (30%), Practice Administrator/DSO Operations (10%)

**Discovery Questions:**
1. "What is your current no-show rate, and what does an unfilled hygiene slot cost you?"
2. "How many patient calls does your front desk handle in a typical day, and what's the average hold time?"
3. "Walk me through your recall process — how do you identify and reach out to overdue patients?"
4. "What percentage of your treatment plans get approved but never scheduled?"
5. "How much time does insurance verification consume for your team each week?"
6. "Can patients book appointments outside of office hours today?"
7. "If you could automate one thing that would keep your chairs full, what would it be?"

**Common Objections:**
1. "We already have Dentrix/Eaglesoft reminders." → Basic reminders are passive; SAOS creates *conversational* automation that handles responses
2. "Patients want to talk to a person, not a bot." → The AI handles routine interactions; humans focus on complex cases and relationship-building
3. "Dental software is too expensive already." → SAOS integrates with existing PMS; no rip-and-replace required
4. "Our patients are older — they don't text." → Voice-based AI options available; meet patients where they are
5. "We tried a recall system before and it didn't work." → Most recall tools are blast-and-hope; SAOS is personalized, intelligent, and persistent

## Industry: Food Trucks

**Typical Company Size:** 1–6 employees per truck; $150K–$500K annual revenue per truck. Solo operators: $40K–$90K part-time; established full-time trucks: $150K–$300K; top performers: $250K–$500K+.

**Common Problems:**
1. **Revenue instability** — Income swings with location, weather, season, and event cancellations; no predictable revenue model
2. **Inventory waste** — Over-portioning, bloated menus, and poor forecasting lead to 6–8% higher food costs than menu recipes
3. **Location and permit chaos** — Tracking permits, event bookings, and location schedules is manual and error-prone
4. **No customer retention system** — Most customers are one-time; no loyalty program or re-engagement mechanism
5. **Social media and marketing is ad hoc** — Owners post when they remember; no systematic customer communication about location updates

**Current Tools:**
- Square POS, Toast, Clover, Goodfynd, Menumium, Sapaad, HelixDash, Tabrick, Deelo, Dewx, Instagram/Facebook, Google Maps, spreadsheets, QuickBooks, FreshBooks

**Where Tools Fail:**
- POS systems track sales but don't connect to inventory or location scheduling
- No integrated platform for location updates, event bookings, and pre-orders
- Inventory is tracked manually or not at all; no predictive ordering
- Customer data sits in POS with no follow-up or loyalty automation
- Social media posting about daily locations is manual and inconsistent
- No automated pre-order or event booking system

**Automation Opportunities:**
1. **Location-based customer notifications** — Auto-post daily location to social media, SMS, and website; geo-targeted push notifications
2. **Inventory prediction and auto-ordering** — Analyze sales patterns to predict prep needs, reduce waste, auto-generate supplier orders
3. **Event booking and permit tracking automation** — Calendar integration for events, auto-renewal reminders for permits, vendor application tracking
4. **Customer loyalty and re-engagement automation** — Capture customer data from POS, send location-specific offers, reward repeat visits
5. **Weather-based operational alerts** — Auto-notify customers of closures, reschedule events, adjust prep based on forecast

**Expected ROI:**
- Reduce food waste by 15–25% through predictive inventory
- Increase repeat customer rate from near-zero to 20–30%
- Save 5–10 hours/week on social media and location updates
- Boost event booking conversion by 40% through automated follow-up
- Improve profit margins from typical 20–25% to 30%+

**Best Offer:** SAOS **Starter Tier** ($1,500–$2,500/mo). Food trucks are lean operations with thin margins; they need lightweight, affordable automation that connects POS, social media, and inventory without enterprise complexity. Growth Tier only if multi-truck fleet.

**Buyer Persona:** Owner/Operator of a food truck doing $150K–$400K, passionate about food but drowning in operations, inventory, and marketing.

**Decision Maker:** Owner/Operator (90%), Co-Founder/Spouse (10%)

**Discovery Questions:**
1. "How do you currently decide how much food to prep for tomorrow, and how often do you run out or throw things away?"
2. "When you change location for the day, how do your customers find out?"
3. "How many of your customers are repeat visitors vs. one-time?"
4. "What's your process for booking events and catering gigs — is it systematic or ad hoc?"
5. "How much time do you spend on social media and location updates each week?"
6. "What happens when bad weather cancels an event you've committed to?"
7. "If you could automate one part of running your truck, what would disappear first?"

**Common Objections:**
1. "Food trucks are too small for automation." → Food trucks are *exactly* the size where automation replaces your most expensive resource: your time
2. "I can't afford another monthly bill." → At $150K–$300K revenue, saving 10–15 hours/week and reducing waste by 15% pays for automation quickly
3. "My customers follow me on Instagram — that's enough." → Instagram reaches followers when they happen to check; automation reaches them when you're *open* and *nearby*
4. "I don't have time to learn new software." → SAOS builds and manages automations for you; you don't become a software expert
5. "I like the personal touch — automation feels corporate." → Automation handles the repetitive logistics; you stay focused on the food and the customers
